title: Piloting

The first step is to pilot the workshops with existing teams, learn from their experience and refine the approach before scaling up facilitation.

## First pilot sequence

**Roman numerals → meeting-room booking → Elevator Saga → archipelago parcel network**

That is, the [foundations](/workshops/foundations/), [shared AI](/workshops/shared-ai/), [under pressure](/workshops/under-pressure/) and [masterclass](/workshops/masterclass/) workshops. This allows early pilots without first building the proposed [games](/games/). Pilot the [leadership workshop](/workshops/leading/) once suitable integrated workstreams and enough participants are available.

## What to prepare for each exercise

- A thin, working starter environment and one worked example.
- A participant brief with explicit initial rules.
- Facilitator-only requirements, staged changes and distinguishing examples.
- A timer, role materials and a visible decision-record format.
- Saved checkpoints and known finale outputs.
- Debrief prompts and an evaluation approach.
- Arrangements for accessibility, consent, interruptions and tool access.

Keep the exercise small enough to expose the intended learning rather than set-up friction.

## What to observe

Collect short confidence ratings, relevant timings, decision artifacts and facilitator observations. Treat them as learning evidence, never as individual performance rankings.

Look for participation, transfer of understanding, unverified assumptions, recovery from absence, response to changed rules and the quality of handover.

Debrief the differences between what the team believed, what it demonstrated and what it left uncertain.

Use findings to adjust scope, timing, role rules, prompts and disturbances. Don't generalise from one team's experience, and don't treat proposed timings, scoring systems or AI practices as validated until pilots support them.

## Questions for pilots to answer

- Which [outcomes](/outcomes/) are primary for each workshop, and what evidence is practical to collect?
- Is TDD a required capability in every relevant session, a learning mechanism, or both?
- What depth of technical understanding is appropriate for each audience and task?
- Which scripted interventions reliably create learning without overloading the session?
- Which practices transfer to normal work, and what helps them persist?
