Exercises, role rules, AI arrangements and games are ways of practising these capabilities, not the outcomes themselves.

## Overall objective

> Help teams make, implement and revise decisions together without losing understanding, agency or accountability as work becomes more complex and AI does more of the implementation.

The programme aims to develop collective judgment, shared ownership and distributed leadership in AI-assisted delivery, while keeping a place for non-AI technical practice.

Finishing a challenge, generating code quickly, following rotations or using one shared agent does not by itself show success.

## Team and leadership outcomes

| Outcome | Capability | Observable evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shared understanding | Understand the problem, important decisions and limitations beyond the current operator | Several participants explain behavior, assumptions, rejected alternatives and the next change |
| Meaningful participation | Let different expertise influence the work | Examples, questions, domain knowledge and dissent affect decisions; contributions aren't judged by typing or speaking time |
| Continuity | Carry on through absence, rotation and changing responsibilities | Returning or replacement participants resume using shared artifacts and team context, not a private conversation |
| Collective judgment | Challenge assumptions and generated solutions with examples and evidence | Participants separate requirements from defaults, predict behavior, test claims and revise decisions when evidence changes |
| Distributed leadership | Make appropriate decisions without constant lead involvement | Teams understand goals, authority, dependencies and escalation; work continues while the lead is away |

### Shared understanding

Participants can explain what the system is meant to do, why significant choices were made, what remains uncertain, and what evidence supports accepting it.

Not everyone needs to know every implementation detail. But the team needs enough understanding to challenge and change the work without depending on its original author or AI conversation.

### Meaningful participation

Driver rotation creates opportunities but doesn't guarantee influence. Domain examples, counterexamples, research, facilitation and spotting uncertainty are all substantial contributions.

Look for whether quieter participants, and people in different roles, can change the team's direction — not whether everyone speaks for the same length of time.

### Continuity

Shared goals, examples, decision records and working software should support handover and recovery from interruptions. Briefings are fine, but nobody should be the sole keeper of the team's reasoning.

### Collective judgment

The team evaluates both what the AI produced and whether the requested behavior represents the problem.

Confidence should be calibrated against evidence: predict behavior for unfamiliar cases, then compare with what actually happens. Less unjustified confidence can be a sign of progress. Passing tests are evidence about the behavior they cover, not proof that requirements are complete or correct.

### Distributed leadership

Leads create direction, decision boundaries, integration expectations and feedback loops, rather than approve every action. Technical coherence and delivery facilitation are different responsibilities, even when one person does both.

## Facilitator outcome

Develop facilitators who can reproduce the learning outcomes without reproducing the original facilitator's personality or exact delivery style. See [training facilitators](/facilitating/training/).

## Ownership and collaboration boundaries

Shared ownership includes understanding and looking after the problem, the code and the resulting service. Owning a domain doesn't remove the need to maintain or diagnose the implementation.

Shared understanding is different from decision authority and accountability. A team can understand and challenge a decision without everyone having a veto over it.

Mobbing isn't the answer to every task. Teams should be able to choose synchronous collaboration, pairing, individual investigation or bounded parallel work while keeping shared ownership and useful integration.

One shared coding agent is a workshop arrangement to investigate, not an outcome or a universal prescription.

## Evidence beyond the workshop

The goal is better everyday delivery, not just good workshop performance.

After a session, ask the team to try a small practice on real work and review how it goes over time — for example shared examples before prompting, tiny operational changes, or a short decision record.

Watch whether understanding spreads, assumptions become visible, handovers improve and the team responds better to change. Keep immediate workshop evidence apart from lasting changes in habits.

Use observations and confidence ratings for learning, never for ranking individuals, and don't generalise from one team's experience.
