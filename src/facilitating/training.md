> Develop facilitators who can reproduce the learning outcomes without reproducing the original facilitator's personality or exact delivery style.

## Start with scripted sessions

A new facilitator shouldn't need to design an exercise or build an environment before running the standard workshop. Each standard session should eventually include:

- a preparation checklist and a runnable starter set-up
- a participant brief
- a timed script with suggested wording and questions
- role rotations and staged requirements
- expected observations
- fallback material
- a debrief guide

Explain the principle behind each intervention, so facilitators understand its purpose rather than just follow instructions. Make clear which parts are essential learning mechanics and which are optional style, and mark the points where adaptation is expected.

## A pathway

1. Take part in a workshop.
2. Observe an experienced facilitator, using an observation guide.
3. Co-facilitate a bounded section.
4. Run a pilot, with feedback.
5. Reflect, revise and demonstrate independent facilitation.

Facilitators can start with the standard script, then adapt it or create their own sessions as their confidence grows. Adaptation is an option, not an entry requirement.

## What facilitators learn to do

- Prepare and run a reliable standard session.
- Explain the purpose of its learning mechanics.
- Invite meaningful participation, including from quieter people, and handle disagreement respectfully.
- Use questions without supplying every decision.
- Maintain consent during interruption and discomfort exercises.
- Recover from unexpected AI output or technical failure.
- Debrief what was observed without forcing a predetermined conclusion.
- Adapt sessions, or create new ones, when ready.

The aim isn't just entertaining sessions, but helping teams carry collaborative habits into everyday delivery. See the [intended outcomes](/outcomes/).
